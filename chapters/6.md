---
title: The Post Office
---

Beyond the front door, the world. Couriers, telegrams, parcels and catalogues,
all of it reachable by a slip of paper.

Hobbs sent a courier to fetch a page from another house, `curl`. He posted a
reply-paid card to check that a cousin's house still answered, `ping`. He
spoke through the long brass tube that runs to the study of a house in the
country, `ssh`, and everything he said there happened there. He packed the carrier's
hamper, `rsync`, so the country house received only the sheets that had changed.
And at the post office counter, `gh`, he filled in telegraph forms for the house
that keeps the shared letter book.

Then he opened the supplier's catalogue. `pip install`, `npm install`: a
parcel of tools, ready to order. Most suppliers are honest. A few print names
that look almost exactly like the honest ones.

You checked the spelling, letter by letter, before you signed.

The next morning a parcel came that you had not ordered. It was a book,
borrowed from a friend, with every one of her letters copied inside.
