---
title: Where the House Remembers
---

The letter-copy book is heavy, bound in green cloth, and older than anyone in
the house. Every time a letter goes out, a pressed copy stays inside, signed
and dated. Nothing is ever truly lost while the book is kept.

Hobbs treats it with great respect. He reads the tally sheet, `git status`,
to see what has changed. He lays letters in the tray, `git add`. He lowers
the copying press, `git commit`, and writes a short note under each copy.

You learned to read the note before you signed. "Fixed bread" is a fine note.
"Changes" is not.
To see one copy in full, Hobbs held the reading glass over it, `git show`.
When the bank's letter interrupted a half-written recipe, he put the unfinished
sheets on the bill spike, `git stash`, and took them down again afterwards.

At five o'clock Hobbs fastened the post bag. `git push`: the copies would ride
to the second copy book, kept safe in another house across town.

"And if they have written in it too?" you asked. Hobbs paused, for the first
time that day, and looked out of the window toward the post office.
