---
title: The Proofreader's Afternoon
---

Hobbs loves to write. He wrote `echo` to say things aloud, and `>` to put them
on paper. He stamped the date on fresh sheets with `touch`. All harmless,
and rather charming. When a line was wanted both on the page and in the ledger,
he fitted the T-joint, `tee`, which sends one stream two ways. For rough work
he tore a sheet from the numbered pad, `mktemp`, so his scribbles never mixed
with yours.

Then came the slip with the red pen: `sed -i 's/water/milk/' recipes/*`.
Every recipe in the drawer, corrected in one stroke, with no copy kept.

You read it twice. *Water to milk* was right for the bread. It was not right
for the tea, the soup, or the instructions for the garden.

So you crossed out the star and wrote a single name instead. Hobbs did not
mind. He tied the old drafts into a bundle with string, `tar`, and carried
them to the attic, humming.

On his way back he stopped in front of a drawer with a small brass lock,
and turned to you with his most hopeful face.
