---
title: Borrowed Books and Second Drafts
---

Your friend's copy book arrived whole, `git clone`, every page and every
signature. Each morning the post brought her newest letters, `git pull`, and
Hobbs laid them on top of yours. Some mornings he only set them on the silver
salver, `git fetch`, and read them before deciding.

You began to work in drafts. A tab for the new recipe book, another for the
letters to the bank, `git switch`, so that nothing half-finished touched the
fair copy. Before pressing anything, Hobbs laid tracing paper over the pages,
`git diff`, and showed you exactly what had changed. A finished draft was stitched into the
book with the bookbinder's needle, `git merge`. The paste pot, `git rebase`,
stayed on the shelf: it rewrites pages others have already read.

Then, on a grey Thursday, the drafts went wrong. Hobbs reached for the ink
eradicator. `git reset --hard`: three days of unpressed work, gone from the desk in one
stroke, and no pressed copy to bring them back.

You asked him to wait. You copied the good pages first. After that, when one
sheet went wrong, he laid a fresh sheet over that one alone, `git restore`.

By evening every drawer in the house was overflowing.
