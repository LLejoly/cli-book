---
title: The Bread That Would Not Rise
---

The cook sent word that the morning loaf had come out flat as a slate.
Hobbs was delighted. A puzzle.

He did not guess. He asked to read. `head` for the first lines of the recipe,
`tail` for the last entries in the kitchen ledger, `wc` to count what had been
written. You signed each slip without a second thought: they only look.
The long ledger he read one page at a time, `less`. Over the grocer's printed
form he laid a cut-out card, `jq`, so that only the boxes he wanted showed.
And the rotary sifter, `rg`, went through every drawer at once for *water*.

Then he laid two recipes side by side, yours and Grandmother's, and asked for
`diff`. Two candlesticks, and between them one line that differed:
*350ml water* against *300ml water*, and a spoon of honey nobody had copied.

"Reading is free," Mrs. Finch had written in the margin. "Read twice, change once."

By noon the bread was fixed, and Hobbs was already sharpening a pen.
He had found other recipes with the wrong amount, and he wanted to correct
them all. At once.
